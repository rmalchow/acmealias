title: acmealias — a hosted acme-dns-compatible challenge responder, for domains whose DNS you don't administer

hi all — long-time reader of this forum, first time announcing something on it. i've built a small service and i'd rather have it torn apart here than anywhere else, because this is where the people who know what's wrong with it are.

the problem it addresses is the one that comes up on this forum every week: dns-01 is the only route to a wildcard, dns-01 wants an api credential for the zone, and the credential your dns provider hands you is almost never scoped to a single record. it can rewrite the apex, the mx records, everything. so it ends up in the acme client's config on the machine that terminates tls, and in that machine's backups, and the sensible thing to do about that is the thing nobody does.

the answer is the challenge-delegation pattern this community has been recommending for years: `_acme-challenge.example.com` is a cname into a zone somebody else runs, and that somebody answers the txt. the credential the acme client holds afterwards can set one record for one name and is worth nothing to a thief. acme-dns (https://github.com/joohoi/acme-dns) is the reference implementation of exactly this and i want to be clear up front that acmealias is not a better idea than acme-dns — it is the same idea, hosted, because the thing standing between acme-dns and wider adoption is that using it means running an authoritative nameserver on a public ip with glue at your registrar, and keeping it up on renewal day.

so: you add a domain, the service assigns it an alias label, you publish one cname by hand, once. after that the client tells us the challenge value at issuance and renewal time, we serve it as a txt for the duration and drop it afterwards, and you never touch dns again for that domain.

the parts that might interest this forum specifically:

- it speaks acme-dns's `/update` protocol, so acme.sh, certify the web, cert-manager's acme-dns solver and the certbot plugin work against it unmodified. it also speaks lego's `httpreq` provider, which is what traefik and caddy's lego builds want.
- `POST /register` is deliberately not implemented the way acme-dns defines it. in acme-dns anyone who can reach the endpoint can mint an account and a subdomain; here the account exists first and registration happens in the console or the api, under a key with permissions. that's a real incompatibility with acme-dns clients that expect to self-register, and i'd rather state it than have someone discover it.
- alias labels are 80 bits of random base32 by default rather than derived from the domain. our nameservers are public, so a derivable label would turn the zone into a membership oracle — anyone could ask whether `shop.example.com` is a customer and get a free answer. there's a setting that derives the label from the name instead, which is legible in a log line and fine for a single organisation, and wrong for a shared one.
- a wildcard and its apex validate at the same challenge name, since the `*.` label is stripped before the name is formed. so `example.com` and `*.example.com` share one alias and one cname, and adding one can add the other in the same action rather than leaving somebody to discover the collision at renewal time.
- there's no cleanup notion in the acme-dns protocol and there is one in `httpreq`, which the service handles rather than papering over; multiple values coexisting at one name during a multi-node renewal is expected and supported, because each node of a load balancer runs acme independently.
- we never hold a private key — not the certificate's, not the acme account's. the only thing we store on your behalf is a label and, for the length of a renewal, a txt value that is public by construction.

the honest state of things. it runs and it issues real certificates. there is pricing on the site — personal accounts free and staying free, team accounts 39 ct per challenge answered with a 199 eur monthly cap, out of prepaid credit rather than a subscription — but no payment processor behind it and no self-registration, so nothing can actually be bought today. the numbers are sized to cover nameservers and a database rather than to make anyone rich. a self-hostable build is intended and doesn't exist yet.

what i'd most like from this forum is the failure modes i haven't hit. rate limits and how i should be advising people to think about them, the propagation-check behaviour of clients i haven't tested, anything about the cname pattern that bites at scale in ways a small deployment never shows. tell me what's wrong with it.
